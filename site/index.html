Portland's narrow streets

A two-way roadway in a residence district, not more than 18 feet wide at any point between two intersections, is a narrow residential roadway under ORS 801.368, and the statutory speed limit on it is 15 mph. This is every Portland block that could be one: two-way, in a residence district, and not an arterial.

What counts as a block

A centerline segment is not a block. The source splits runs mid-block at jurisdiction and address-range breaks — 28,970 of 112,070 segments touch a node where exactly two streets meet, which is a continuation, not an intersection. Blocks here are rebuilt from network topology, so 39,103 Portland segments become 34,379 blocks.

Where the mileage is

Read as "18 feet or less somewhere", 100.8 miles already qualify. Read strictly as "never wider than 18 feet", it is 63.4. No Oregon appellate decision, Attorney General opinion or ODOT guidance construing the statute was found, so both are one click apart rather than one being asserted.

Beyond that is the striping inventory. ORS 801.450 measures the roadway "exclusive of the shoulder", so a block wider than 18 ft can be brought inside the statute with an edge line. Switch to never wider and push the width past 18 to see it: 256.6 miles sit at 24 ft or less, which is 3 ft of shoulder each side, out of 1,154.6 eligible miles in total. PBOT's city traffic engineer rejects paint for this purpose; PBOT's own Pedestrian Design Guide allows it.

Narrowings

PBOT records one width per pavement section, so a bioswale or a curb extension is invisible to it. Measuring curb to curb every 5 ft finds 783 places a street is materially narrower than itself, 150 of them reaching 18 ft or less. SE Taylor at SE 50th is on record as a flat 30 ft; a pair of bioswales pinches it to 20.3 ft.

Under the narrow somewhere reading those count, so a block's narrowest point is the lower of PBOT's record and the curb measurement. 202 blocks (20.7 miles) qualify on a narrow point only the curb lines can see, and the readout marks them. Only stations with street curb on both sides count: a shoulder line can sit inside the roadway rather than at its edge, and counting those would add 11 miles of streets that are not narrow.

These are floors, not totals

13,974 blocks — 1,067 miles — have no PBOT pavement width at all, and the gap sits in exactly the local and unimproved streets where narrow ones live. Measuring curb to curb every 5 ft reaches 542 of those miles, and those blocks are on this map with their width marked as coming from the curb lines. The remaining 525 miles are measured by nobody, so the totals here are a floor.
