ORS 801.368 · narrow residential roadway

Portland's narrow streets

A two-way roadway in a residence district, not more than 18 feet wide at any point between two intersections, is a narrow residential roadway, and the statutory speed limit on it is 15 mph. This is every block in Portland measured against that sentence.

What counts as a block

A centerline segment is not a block. The source splits runs mid-block at jurisdiction and address-range breaks — 28,970 of 112,070 segments touch a node where exactly two streets meet, which is a continuation, not an intersection.

Blocks here are rebuilt from network topology: node degree over the full four-county network, segments joined at degree-2 nodes, connected components. 39,103 Portland segments become 34,379 blocks.

Two readings of one sentence

"Not more than 18 feet wide at any point" reads as a maximum: a block that is never wider. Under that reading the answer is 51.4 miles.

Read as "narrow somewhere", it is 72.7. Both sliders are provided because no Oregon appellate decision, Attorney General opinion or ODOT guidance construing the statute was found.

Narrowings

PBOT records one width per pavement section, so a bioswale or a curb extension is invisible to it. Measuring curb to curb every 5 ft finds 783 places a street is materially narrower than itself — 150 of them reaching 18 ft or less.

SE Taylor at SE 50th is on record as a flat 30 ft. A pair of bioswales pinches it to 20.3 ft.

These are floors, not totals

A block is testable only when every segment in it has a PBOT pavement width on record. 13,974 blocks — 1,067 miles — cannot be tested at all, and the gap is concentrated in local streets, private roads and driveways, exactly where narrow streets live.

Widths are RoadWidth, the graded roadway. Using PaveWidth instead gives 74.1 miles at 18 ft; the two fields are undocumented, so the reading moves the answer.
