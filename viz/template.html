What counts as a block

A centerline segment is not a block. The source splits runs mid-block at jurisdiction and address-range breaks — 28,970 of 112,070 segments touch a node where exactly two streets meet, which is a continuation, not an intersection.

Blocks here are rebuilt from network topology: node degree over the full four-county network, segments joined at degree-2 nodes, connected components. 39,103 Portland segments become 34,379 blocks.

Why the unit matters

Tested per segment, the 18 ft answer is 64.7 miles. Tested per block, as written, it is 51.4 — a 26% difference.

The gap is 13.3 miles of genuinely narrow roadway that sits in blocks widening somewhere along their length. Turn on narrow but block widens to see it.

These are floors, not totals

A block is testable only when every segment in it has a PBOT pavement width on record. 13,974 blocks — 1,067 miles — cannot be tested at all, and the gap is concentrated in local streets, private roads and driveways, exactly where narrow streets live.

Widths are RoadWidth, the graded roadway. Using PaveWidth instead gives 74.1 miles at 18 ft; the two fields are undocumented, so the reading moves the answer.
